## Current Lab Members

### Dr. Margaret Frank

*Principal Investigator*

![Dr. Margaret Frank](images/people/dr-margaret-frank.jpg)

Margaret is a molecular plant biologist who is broadly interested in understanding how multicellular organisms communicate across tissues and organ systems, and applying this knowledge towards sustainable crop production. Margaret joined Cornell's School of Integrative Plant Science ([SIPS](https://sips.cals.cornell.edu/)) in July 2018. Prior to moving to Cornell, Margaret was an NSF PGRP Postdoctoral Fellow at the Donald Danforth Plant Science Center in St Louis, MO, where she studied the dynamics of graft junction formation and graft-transmissible RNA movement.

Margaret earned her PhD at Cornell, working with [Mike Scanlon](http://scanlonlab.plantbio.cornell.edu/) on the evolution of shoot meristem patterning in early land plant lineages.

When she's not doing science, Margaret loves to run, travel, and drink coffee.

### Michelle Heeney

*Ph.D. Candidate*

![Michelle Heeney](images/people/michelle-heeney.jpg)

Michelle is a PhD student in the Frank Lab and the [Roeder Lab](https://roeder.wicmb.cornell.edu/). She works on dissecting the underpinnings controlling and influencing long distance mRNA movement. Michelle graduated from the University of Massachusetts with two B.S.' one in biology and one in math with a concentration in statistics. Her undergraduate thesis work focused on floral morphology in grasses, under the guidance of [Dr. Madelaine Bartlett](http://www.bartlettlab.org/).

Michelle also worked in the biotech industry at the startup company [Inari Agriculture](https://inari.com/) where she developed her skills of plant transformation and bioinformatics.

Outside of the lab Michelle has a passion for film photography and fine art.

### Elise Boisvert

### Noor AlBader

*Post-Doctoral Researcher*

![Noor AlBader](images/people/noor-albader.jpg)

Noor broadly studies the transcriptional response to abiotic and biotic stressors in plants. Her work has encompassed staple crops such as rice, to novelty organisms such as chia and the beaver. Her genomic work has also led her to work on date palm and wild relatives of rice, with a focus on adaptation to desert and costal regions, respectively, at the Center of Desert Agriculture (KAUST).

She completed her PhD (2019) in the Molecular and Cellular Biology Program at Oregon State University (OSU). She is now a post-doctoral researcher, co-hosted by the Frank and [Nelson](https://btiscience.org/andrew-nelson/) labs.

### Israel Gabay

*Post-Doctoral Researcher*

![Israel Gabay](images/people/israel-gabay.jpg)

Israel is a postdoc investigating the coupling of hydromechanical and electrophysiological processes in mediating long-distance signals in crops. His research aims to enhance our understanding of the inner communication system in plants and leverage this knowledge to guide crop improvement and plant bioengineering.

Israel holds a Ph.D. in mechanical engineering from the Technion - Israel Institute of Technology. He is hosted by the Frank and Stroock labs and supported by the Fulbright Fellowship.

In his free time, Israel enjoys spending time with friends and family and playing soccer.

### Xing Ma

*Laboratory Technician*

![Xing Ma](images/people/xing-ma.jpg)

Xing is a plant pathologist who pinpointed Dickeya dianthicola as the source of potato blackleg and soft rot outbreaks after 2014 in the Northeast US. His work also identified the potential of wild species Solanum microdontum to build better disease resistance into potato crops.

With a PhD from THE OSU and a decade of research experience at Cornell, Xing has deep expertise in pathogen genomics and bioinformatics. When he’s off the clock, you’ll likely find him researching computer parts for his next PC build, playing video games, or catching up on music.
